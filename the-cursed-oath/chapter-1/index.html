Chapter 1 — The Healer with Forbidden Blood

1.1 The Night the Stars Bled

The elders of Lira’s village liked to say that the night sky was a scripture of the gods—unchanging, eternal, immune to mortal sorrow. But on the night everything unraveled, the stars trembled like living things, as if some unseen hand had disturbed the heavens themselves.

Lira noticed it first while fetching water from the river. The pail slipped from her fingers the moment she saw the red shimmer on the surface—thin streaks of crimson light rippling across the dark water. She blinked, thinking it a reflection of torches somewhere in the distance. But there were no torches. No fire. No movement except the quiet, steady rush of the river.

Above her, the sky pulsed.

Not bright. Not loud.

Just a slow, eerie heartbeat of red.

A color no star should ever bleed.

She stepped closer to the riverbank, instinct tightening her chest. The air tasted metallic, like the after-scent of a wound. Her fingertips tingled. Her mother once told her that her bloodline would always react to unnatural magic—even the magic Lira tried so hard to pretend she didn’t have.

She whispered to herself, “It’s starting again.”

The air cooled sharply, brushing cold fingers along the back of her neck. Leaves rustled without wind. The faint hum beneath her skin—one she had fought for years to suppress—began to rise, like a chorus waking from slumber.

Lira pressed her palm against her chest, as if she could silence it.

Not now. Not here. Not when she had tried so hard to live quietly.

A sudden crack split the air.

Not thunder.

A tear.

A rip.

Something breaking open.

Lira spun toward the forest. Light—red, the same sickening red as the river glow—flared between the trees. A streak shot across the sky, trailing sparks like embers torn from a dying fire. It fell rapidly, screaming through the night, and crashed deeper in the woods with a sound that rattled every bone in her body.

For a heartbeat, the world went still.

Then the magic inside her surged.

Not gently.

Not softly.

It roared.

Pain lanced through her arm. Lira hissed, clutching her wrist as heat seared across her palm. When she pulled her hand back, she saw it—a faint crimson sigil burned into her skin. A shape she didn’t recognize but somehow instinctively feared.

“No… no, please—”

The sigil pulsed.

The river shimmered darker.

The stars shuddered overhead like lanterns caught in a gust.

A scream tore through the silence.

A human scream.

Not from the village.

From the woods.

Her breath stilled in her throat. She looked behind her—back toward the dim lights of her home, where the rest of the healers would be preparing their sleeping herbs and poultices. She should run to them. Call for help. Fetch someone braver, stronger, less cursed than she was.

But the screaming didn’t stop.

And she had taken an oath.

No matter how troubled she was by her own blood, she was still a healer. Still responsible for lives. Still someone who could not ignore suffering.

Lira snatched up the fallen pail, tossed the water aside, and hurried toward the forest. Branches clawed at her sleeves as she pushed through the undergrowth. The red glow guided her like a sinister lantern.

The smell hit her first—burnt ozone, iron, and something older than time. She covered her mouth as she approached a small clearing.

A crater smoldered in the center, heat rippling off shattered earth. At the heart of it lay a man.

Or something that resembled one.

He was sprawled half-conscious, armor cracked and smoking as if he had fallen straight from the heavens. His hair, dark and tangled, clung to his face. His breathing was shallow—gasping, labored, and full of pain.

Lira froze at the edge of the crater.

Two thoughts collided in her mind:

He’s dying.

And—

He’s dangerous.

But the oath tugged her forward. She knelt beside him cautiously, scanning the injuries. Burns along his shoulders. Blood seeping from cracked skin. A sigil etched across his chest, glowing faintly with the same red as her own palm.

She reached out—but her fingers hovered just shy of touching him. She wasn’t sure why. Perhaps it was intuition. Perhaps it was terror.

Then his hand shot up.

His fingers clamped weakly around her wrist.

Lira inhaled sharply, but he didn’t pull her closer. He barely had the strength to lift his head. His voice, when it came, was a rasp of ash and thunder.

“You… shouldn’t… be here.”

Lira swallowed hard. “You’re hurt. I can help. Let me—”

His grip tightened.

“No,” he forced out. “They’re coming.”

He coughed, and the motion sent a violent tremor through him. The sigil on his chest flickered, then flared—bright enough to illuminate the entire clearing.

Lira shielded her eyes. “What is that magic? Who are you?”

“Kael,” he whispered. “Last heir… Nightfire House…”

Her heart lurched.

Nightfire.

One of the Three Great Houses.

And the most feared.

Before she could react, another explosion of red light tore across the sky. It streaked toward them like a wounded comet. Kael’s eyes widened with urgent terror.

“No time—”

He grabbed her hand and pressed her palm against his chest.

The same moment the falling light struck the earth.

Magic erupted.

A surge of crimson radiance burst from the point where their skin touched. The sigil on her palm aligned with his. A shockwave blasted through them—painful, blinding, alive.

Lira screamed as heat seared up her arm and into her heart.

Kael convulsed.

Blood rose into the air around them—not dripping, but lifting, swirling, forming a ring of scarlet fire. The stars flickered overhead, dimming one by one.

The bond snapped into place with a force that stole her breath.

Then the world went dark.

Lira didn’t remember falling. Only the cold earth beneath her cheek. The fading glow of the crater. The steady, unfamiliar pulse in her veins.

Not her heartbeat.

His.

Intertwined.

Unwelcome.

Unbreakable.

The last thing she heard before consciousness slipped away was Kael’s murmur, barely audible but heavy with something she didn’t yet understand.

“The curse has chosen.”

1.2 A Patient She Shouldn’t Have Saved

Lira had always believed that healing was a quiet craft, something practiced in soft light and calm breaths, where the world slowed enough for a pair of steady hands to stitch together what fate had torn apart. She worked in the lower infirmary of the city, a place where the sick were brought when their wounds were too strange or too dangerous for ordinary physicians. The stone walls sweated cold, and the lamps flickered as though wary of the shadows lingering in the corners.

She liked it there. The hush, the stillness, the certainty that what she did mattered. No noise of politics, no whispers of lineage, no reminders of what her own blood carried. Only the pulse of life and her hands guiding it back.

That night, however, the infirmary felt different—pressurized, as though the air itself awaited something dreadful.

A knock cracked through the quiet, sharp enough to jolt Lira from her thoughts. Before she could answer, the doors burst open. Two guards staggered in, dragging a man between them. His armor glimmered with a black-hot sheen, like metal warmed by coals that never cooled. Sparks drifted off him in slow motions, curling into the darkness and leaving tiny burns wherever they landed.

“Bring him here,” Lira whispered, though the urgency in her voice betrayed her calm. She stepped closer, instinct curling in her chest. Even unconscious, the man radiated a heat that scraped against her skin. She felt it before she saw it—the humming in her bones, the vibration in the air around him, a rhythm like a drum buried deep underground.

The guards dropped him onto the table with a thud that shook the lanterns.

“He was found at the eastern gate,” one guard panted. “Half-burned, half-frozen, and…” “Alive,” the other muttered. “Which shouldn’t be possible.”

Lira knelt by the stranger. She studied his face. Even beneath the soot and char, his features held an austere beauty—sharp lines carved by storms, a mouth clenched as though even unconsciousness could not loosen it, and lashes dark as ink against skin glowing with residual heat.

“His heart?” Lira asked.

“Still beating,” a guard replied. “But wrong. Like it’s trying to burn its way out.”

Lira exhaled slowly. She reached for the pulse at his throat. The beat thrummed through her fingers, erratic and uneven, as if a wild rhythm refused to be tamed. She felt something else too—an undertone, a vibration not from the body but from the blood itself. A whisper.

She frowned. Blood shouldn’t whisper.

“Step back,” she told the guards. “Both of you.”

They exchanged uncertain looks but obeyed, retreating until the walls held them.

Lira pressed her palm against the man’s chest. Heat surged, prickling along her arm. She swallowed, steadying her breathing. Healing always demanded focus, but this… this demanded something more. Something older.

Her fingers trembled.

“Don’t,” she murmured to herself, though she wasn’t sure what she meant. Don’t listen? Don’t activate it? Don’t remember?

The whisper returned, stronger now, curling around her thoughts. A note of hunger. A note of pain.

She closed her eyes.

Lira felt the world shift.

The infirmary dimmed, shadows stretching like long fingers. The lamps flickered until only a thin glow remained, shimmering around the body on the table. And within that glow, she saw it—the man’s blood. Not red. Not even warm. Instead, it gleamed like molten obsidian, swirling in slow spirals, glowing from within like something alive.

“Stop…” she breathed.

But the blood continued. It reached out, trembling, as though searching for something. Or someone.

Her skin prickled; her heartbeat rose.

“Lira,” a guard whispered behind her. “What is it?”

“Nothing,” she replied quickly, though her voice cracked.

Because she knew very well what it was.

Forbidden magic.

Healing magic born from blood.

She had sworn never to touch it again.

But the man’s pulse faltered. Then steadied. Then shuddered violently.

“Please,” she whispered—not to the guards, not to the man, but to the quiet space inside her where she locked away the memory of the last time she used it. “Please, don’t make me.”

And still… the whisper called.

Her hand moved on its own.

Lira inhaled sharply, drawing the strange warmth toward her, letting it mingle with her own blood. The and then she felt it—a familiar hum pulsing through her veins, a resonance like a string tightened, then released.

Her magic awakened.

A thin scar along her wrist glowed.

The man inhaled.

His chest rose, the rhythm smoothing, inch by inch.

“Lira!” “Stop—!”

The guards shouted, but their voices blurred.

Because something had changed.

The healing wasn’t flowing outward.

It was looping back.

Connecting.

Binding.

Her eyes flickered open.

The man’s glowing blood surged toward her fingertips, entangling in delicate strands—threads of obsidian light. They curled around her, tugged gently, testing, reaching.

She jerked back.

Too late.

A crackling pulse exploded between them, a burst of cold and heat swirling together. Lira gasped, gasping as the sensation knifed through her chest, then settled, heavy and warm, like an anchor embedded deep inside her heart.

The man’s eyes snapped open.

They glowed—not with flame, but with a deep, bruised amber, as though embers lay buried far beneath ice.

Kael.

Lira didn’t know his name then, but she felt his gaze strike her like lightning.

“Who…” he whispered, voice rasping, “touched me?”

Her throat tightened.

“I— I healed you,” she breathed, though the words felt inadequate. “I shouldn’t have… I didn’t mean—”

“You did,” he whispered, and the intensity in his eyes deepened. “You bind me.”

The threads of glowing blood tightened between them, pulling gently, like a tether.

Lira’s breath caught.

“No,” she murmured. “No, this can’t be happening.”

Because in the realm of the Bloodbound, there were only two ways a bond formed:

By ritual.

Or by fate.

And fate had never been kind to her.

“Lira,” Kael said again, though he had never heard her name before. “What have you done?”

She could only stare at the shimmering strands connecting them, trembling as if they were alive.

What she had done… was save a patient she should never have touched.

And in doing so, she had awakened a curse older than memory.

And bound herself to a man whose blood burned like nightfire.

And whose destiny would now entwine with hers forever.

1.3 Whispers of Blood-Singing

Shadows in the Quiet

Lira had never meant to listen.

She had been trained to ignore the soft murmuring that curled at the edges of her hearing, the strange vibrations that trembled through her veins whenever she worked near wounded flesh. Healing, her teachers had insisted, required calm, control, and silence. But silence had always abandoned her. Instead, something else lingered — a low, trembling hum that rose and fell like a distant choir buried beneath stone.

Tonight it felt closer.

She sat alone in the infirmary, moonlight spilling through a cracked window and painting silver shapes across the floor. The patient she’d treated earlier — a soldier with a shattered arm — slept fitfully, his breathing shallow but steady. Lira had completed the healing, binding bone and flesh with a careful technique she’d learned from her mother. Yet the hum remained, coiling in her chest.

It wasn’t a sound so much as a sensation.

A warmth that fluttered in her throat, a pulse in her fingertips, a rhythm that matched the throb of her heart. She inhaled slowly, trying to steady herself, but the whispers only grew stronger — softer, yes, but sharper, too, like threads of silk dragged across glass.

She pressed her hand to the stone wall beside her, feeling a faint vibration beneath the cold surface. It wasn’t coming from the infirmary, or even the building. It seeped through the structure, through the earth, through something older. And it called to her.

The First Note

Lira closed her eyes.

When she did, the whispers sharpened. Not words — nothing her mind could translate — but a pattern, a pulse, a cadence. A rhythm that felt as though it had been written inside her long before she’d ever understood what it meant. She let the sound flow through her, hesitant, frightened, yet irresistibly drawn.

It brushed against her consciousness like a hand she couldn’t see.

Then, suddenly, a single tone rose above the rest. Clear. Pure. Terrifying.

Her breath caught.

The tone wasn’t external. It wasn’t coming from the walls, or the stone, or the night. It rose from her own bones, vibrating in the hollow spaces between her ribs. She gasped and clutched her chest, startled by the intensity.

The air around her trembled.

And with it came a subtle warmth — the warmth of something awakening.

Lira staggered back, her heel catching on the wooden stool behind her. She barely kept her balance as the whispering chorus surged, swelling until it felt as though every heartbeat was tearing loose a fragment of something ancient.

Memories flickered.

Not hers — not fully — but impressions. Shadows. Echoes. A buried history buried so deeply she had never suspected it existed. She saw flashes of red light, the gleam of a blade, the glimmer of a sigil etched in blood, a circle of warriors kneeling in a ritual older than kingdoms.

Then the vision collapsed.

A Taste of the Past

Lira breathed in sharply.

Her throat burned, as though she’d swallowed a spark. Her palms tingled, and the hum softened again, retreating into the quiet like a tide slipping back from the shore. But the aftermath lingered — a residue, a warmth, a knowledge she couldn’t name.

She wiped her hands on her skirt, though they felt clean. The sensation wasn’t physical — it was deeper, rooted somewhere between emotion and instinct.

“What… was that?” she whispered.

Her voice trembled.

No answer came.

Only the faint heartbeat of the infirmary around her, the rustling of the curtains, the shallow breaths of the wounded soldier, the whisper of wind outside.

But the hum… the hum lingered still, flickering in the corners of her awareness like a candle about to gutter out.

A memory pressed gently against her mind. Something she wasn’t ready to understand.

A name.

A shape.

A word formed of light and darkness together.

Then it vanished.

Lira sank weakly onto the stool, grasping the edge with trembling fingers. She wasn’t afraid of the whispering — not exactly. It wasn’t a threat. It felt… familiar, almost comforting, like a melody she’d heard long ago and forgotten.

But she had no reason for it to be familiar.

Her healing magic, though forbidden by the council, had always been quiet, subtle, restrained. She’d learned to focus on techniques, on herbal compounds, on disciplined control.

Not this.

Not the wild pulse of something older and untamed.

A Call She Didn’t Understand

Lira inhaled again, slower this time.

“Why now?” she murmured.

The answer rose in the hush of her thoughts, not in words but in a shift in the atmosphere — a subtle tightening, a pressure behind her eyes, a tug deep in her blood.

Something had reached for her.

Something had begun.

She hadn’t chosen it. She hadn’t even knowingly practiced it.

But the whispering felt like an invitation, or a warning, or a memory pulled from a forgotten age.

A ship drifting in the dark.

A flame relit.

A bond waiting.

Lira’s hands trembled as she pressed them together, closing her eyes again.

Every part of her body seemed to lean toward the sound, as though gravity itself had changed direction.

She shivered.

Something warm curled around her heart.

And for a moment — just a moment — she felt as though she wasn’t alone.

Then the hum vanished completely.

Gone.

Only silence.

And an emptiness where the warmth had been.

Lira exhaled shakily.

Whatever had whispered to her… it had been only the beginning.

And she somehow knew — even without understanding why — that the whispers would return.

Sooner than she wanted.

And when they did, her life would no longer be her own.

1.4 The Mark on Lira’s Palm

A warmth she did not expect

Lira had never been afraid of blood. She had spent her life surrounded by it—cuttings from herbs, the spill of a wounded soldier, the slow pulse of life slipping through torn flesh. Blood was a language she understood, a story she could read even when others could not. But the mark blooming on her palm that night felt nothing like the familiar warmth of healing. It throbbed with a heat that curled inward, as if something alive had awakened beneath her skin and pressed its tiny, burning heart against her own.

She stared at it in the dim glow of the lamps, her fingers trembling. The symbol formed in sharp, dark lines—intricate, twisting, almost beautiful in a way that made her stomach twist. It looked older than any script she had ever studied, older than any magic she had seen. And it glimmered faintly, as though catching light that wasn’t there.

Lira exhaled slowly, fighting the strange dizziness creeping up her spine. She pressed her palm against the wooden table, but the warmth still pulsed, rhythmical and insistent, like a heartbeat trying to teach her a new pattern.

A bond she never meant to forge

“I didn’t do anything,” she whispered to herself, though she wasn’t entirely sure what she meant. She hadn’t intended to bind herself to the warrior. She hadn’t even meant to touch him with healing blood, not in the way she had. But Kael’s wounds had been collapsing, the dark flames searing through his veins, and her instincts had taken over. She’d reached for him before thought, before caution, before fear. And something had answered.

The mark on her palm glowed again, stronger this time, spilling a thin ribbon of silver along the lines. Lira jerked her hand back, startled, and the light flickered like a candle caught in a sudden draft.

Her breath hitched. “Bloodbound,” she murmured, the word slipping out before she could stop it.

She had heard stories—the kind whispered in medicine halls and taverns, half-truths meant to frighten apprentices into obedience. Ancient warriors, tied together by curses and devotion. Bonds forged in rituals so old even the oldest texts had forgotten their names. Bonds that could not be broken, even by death.

But she had never believed them.

Until now.

Pain, but not her own

A sudden stab—sharp and unexpected—shot through her wrist. It wasn’t her pain. She knew it instantly. Her body reacted, yes, but something deeper, something buried in the bond, flared in response to a hurt far away.

She gasped and clutched her wrist, breath sputtering. “What… what is this?”

Another pulse. Another distant ache, like a knife turned inside a body she could not see.

Kael.

She didn’t know how she knew it, but she did. He had been close when the binding happened, close enough for her senses to merge with his in that split moment. Now the bond held them together across the space of the room, across the space of the world, tying their pain like threads of a tapestry.

She swallowed hard, forcing herself to breathe.

“Stop,” she whispered, though she wasn’t speaking to Kael. She spoke to the pain, to the magic, to the strange trembling that flooded her nerves.

A symbol older than memory

Lira looked again at her palm, now steadier. The mark had cooled, but the lines remained. She traced one with a trembling fingertip. It felt etched—like something carved rather than drawn—and the surface hummed faintly under her touch.

It felt cold now, but not dead. Cold like a blade waiting in a sheath.

She sat back, her eyes widening as understanding whispered through her. The symbol wasn’t random. It wasn’t ornate decoration meant to show rank or prestige.

It was a seal.

A binding.

A contract.

Something meant to connect two beings until one or both were destroyed.

Her throat tightened. “And it’s mine,” she murmured. “And his.”

Her eyes flickered toward the doorway, where Kael lay unconscious, his body still trembling with leftover embers of the nightfire curse. She had seen the scars, the deep blackened veins and the smoldering glow beneath his skin. She had felt his pain before she healed him.

But she had never expected this.

A quiet fear

Lira pressed her palm to her chest, feeling her heartbeat accelerate. The room felt suddenly too quiet, the shadows gathering in corners as though listening. She had always believed fear came from uncertainty, but this… this fear came from certainty.

The mark meant something. Something dangerous. Something ancient.

She inhaled deeply, trying to steady herself. Her world had shifted in a single moment—the kind of moment that rewrites a life.

And she had no idea how to undo it.

“What have I done?” she whispered, though the question felt heavier than the air around her, sinking into her bones.

A pulse returns

Then, softly, almost gently, the mark warmed again.

A slow pulse.

Not pain this time. Not fear. Something else.

Connection.

She froze.

Because this warmth wasn’t hers.

And it wasn’t his pain.

It felt like a reaching.

A presence.

A thread stretching between them.

Her breath caught.

“Kael…”

She wasn’t sure if she called to him, or if the bond called to both of them.

But in that trembling moment, with the silver lines glowing faintly against her skin, Lira understood one truth with perfect clarity:

The mark wasn’t just a curse.

It was a beginning.

1.5 When the Hunters Arrive

Cold Footsteps in the Dark

The night had settled over the village like a shroud, heavy and still, pressing against the wooden roofs and cobbled paths until even the air seemed unwilling to move. Lira stood inside the small clinic, hands trembling slightly as she wiped dried blood from her palms. The healing she had performed earlier still throbbed in her veins—an ache unlike anything she’d ever felt, a pulse that whispered with a rhythm older than language.

Outside, the wind shifted. A sound drifted in, muffled but unmistakable—footsteps. Not the casual steps of travelers or villagers returning home. These were measured, disciplined. And many.

Lira’s breath caught.

She wasn’t supposed to be noticed. She wasn’t supposed to draw attention. Healing magic, when used improperly, burned through the body, leaving traces only certain people knew how to follow. She had used it anyway. For a dying man who had begged her with eyes full of fear and hope. And now the consequences were beginning to gather.

“Not now,” she whispered, though the words felt powerless. “Please… not now.”

The clinic door rattled, the wooden frame trembling under a gentle knock. Not a polite knock, but a warning. A signal. Lira stepped back instinctively, her heart hammering, the strange warmth of the bloodbond—still newly formed, still unsteady—glowing beneath her skin.

Another knock came, sharper this time.

Then the sound of boots against stone.

Shadows at the Threshold

Lira moved toward the window, careful not to let the curtains shift. She pressed her eye to the gap and saw them: figures clad in dark armor, their silhouettes sharp and angular beneath the firelight from a lantern held aloft. Their helmets bore the sigil of the Hunters—an ancient order tasked with tracking magical anomalies, especially healing blood. And especially those who wielded it without sanction.

They weren’t here for a conversation.

One of them stepped forward, raising a hand in a signal. Others fanned out, encircling the building with the precision of soldiers who had practiced this dance a thousand times. Their formation tightened, their boots crunching softly on the frozen gravel.

Lira felt the cold seep through her bones.

“They’ve found me,” she murmured.

She wasn’t wrong. Something in her magic—something wild, something forbidden—had bled into the night, glowing like a beacon. She hadn’t meant to, of course. She’d been focused on saving a life, not hiding her own. But the Hunters didn’t care about intent. They cared about control. And order. And eliminating anything that threatened the fragile balance between the Houses.

A metallic clatter echoed near the door.

They had placed something there—perhaps a device meant to detect magic, or a simple tool meant to breach. Lira swallowed hard.

Her thoughts raced. She could try to flee through the back. She could try to hide. She could try to… but where? And for how long?

She was trapped.

The Door Splinters

A crack split the quiet as the Hunters rammed the door.

Wood groaned. Hinges screamed.

“Open in the name of the Order,” a voice called out, filtered through a mask and cold as the winter wind.

Lira clenched her fists. The warmth inside her surged again—an instinctive pulse, almost protective. She felt it even before she understood it, like a spark catching in dry tinder.

She wasn’t alone anymore.

Then the door burst inward.

Fragments of wood scattered across the floor as the Hunters stormed inside, weapons poised, eyes scanning, searching. Their movements were flawless, coordinated, trained.

Lira stepped back, raising her hands slowly.

“I’m not a threat,” she said. Her voice shook slightly, though she tried to steady it. “I used healing magic because—”

“Because,” one Hunter interrupted, “you broke the laws of the Houses. Healing blood is forbidden without sanction.”

“It was a life-or-death moment,” Lira insisted. “You would have done the same.”

“I,” the Hunter replied, “don’t take risks with forbidden arts.”

Another Hunter stepped forward, his gaze sharp, studying her with the clinical intensity of a predator. “Show your hands.”

Lira complied, though every instinct told her not to. She felt the pulse again—stronger now, a thrum of energy that wasn’t her own, a resonance that scraped against her bones.

Then it happened.

A spark of crimson light flickered between her palms.

The Hunters froze.

Every weapon aimed at her in the same breath.

“What is that?” one demanded.

“A sign,” another whispered. “A bond.”

Lira felt the warmth flare, a rush of heat that spread through her chest like wildfire. She gasped, clutching her heart.

“No,” she breathed. “No, this can’t be—”

Because the spark wasn’t simply magic.

It wasn’t simply healing.

It was a binding.

One forged in blood and fate.

And she hadn’t meant to create it.

But the bond had formed anyway.

And somewhere, far from this clinic, someone else felt it too.

A Presence Beyond the Hunters

The Hunters exchanged looks.

“The House will want to see this,” the sharp-eyed one muttered.

“This is a Bloodbound manifestation,” another said.

“Not possible,” a third whispered.

But Lira’s trembling hands glowed brighter, and the warmth inside her surged again, stronger, deeper, trembling with a voice she couldn’t hear but somehow understood.

A voice that belonged to someone powerful.

Someone dangerous.

Someone who had been waiting.

Lira staggered back, breathing hard.

“Please,” she whispered. “Don’t take me. I don’t know what I’ve done.”

One Hunter stepped forward, raising his weapon.

But before he could speak, before he could strike—

A crackling burst of crimson light exploded from Lira’s chest, filling the clinic with a blinding glow.

And in that instant, every Hunter felt it—the weight of something ancient awakening.

Something they had spent generations trying to erase.

Something called the Bloodbound.

1.6 A Warrior Cloaked in Nightfire

A Shape in the Dark

The corridor smelled of cold stone and old ash, the kind of scent that settled into every crack of a palace built on victories and buried regrets. Lira had stepped inside expecting only silence, maybe a glimpse of a wounded soldier or a trembling apprentice. She had not expected him.

At first, he appeared as nothing more than a silhouette—tall, still, and shrouded in a darkness that seemed heavier than the shadows around him. The torchlight behind Lira flickered, catching the edges of his form and revealing a faint ember-like glow clinging to his armor. It wasn’t fire, not exactly, but something close: a trembling warmth that curled around the metal, breathing like a trapped flame.

When he moved, the glow pulsed.

Lira inhaled sharply. She had seen battlefield flames before, wild and uncontrolled, but nothing like this. The glow clung to him the way a secret clung to a tongue—too close, too intimate, impossible to ignore.

He stepped forward, and the warmth reached her skin, though the air around them remained cold. It wasn’t heat that burned; it was something older, something that remembered the ashes of a war long forgotten.

The Weight of a Curse

His eyes found hers, and Lira felt the world tilt. They were the color of molten obsidian, black but reflecting every flicker of light, every trembling flame. She expected anger, or perhaps exhaustion. Instead, she saw something far more unsettling—an emptiness carved by centuries of responsibility.

Kael.

She didn’t yet know his name, but she knew the kind of person he was. You could sense it in the way he held himself, as though every step required a decision he had already made a thousand times before. A warrior shaped not only by battle but by something heavier, something that pressed on his chest the way a hand presses on a wound.

His armor was segmented plates of blackened steel, each etched with symbols Lira didn’t recognize. But it wasn’t the symbols that caught her attention. It was the way the glow seeped from within the metal itself, leaking through tiny cracks like fire trying to escape.

“Are you hurt?” she asked, though she wasn’t sure whether she meant the question or feared it.

He didn’t answer immediately. Instead, he watched her with a kind of detached curiosity, as though he was trying to read a story buried beneath her skin.

The Fire That Wouldn’t Die

When he finally spoke, his voice was low and rough, like gravel pushed together by a slow-moving wind.

“I am not,” he said. “Not in the way you think.”

His gauntlet rose to his chest. The glow surged in response, brightening until it cast orange reflections across the walls.

“It burns,” he murmured.

Lira hesitated. She wasn’t sure whether he meant the fire or the curse. Or perhaps both.

“It always burns,” he continued, as though he had forgotten she was there. “Ever since the night my father died. Ever since the oath took hold.”

He exhaled, and the glow dimmed slightly, curling back into the armor as if embarrassed by its own brightness.

Lira stepped closer, her healer’s instincts rising despite her uncertainty. The warmth radiating from him tugged at her senses, but it didn’t hurt her. Instead, it hummed softly, like a distant drum beat.

“This isn’t normal fire,” she whispered.

“No,” he replied. “It’s a memory.”

A Warrior Born of Flames

He turned, and for a moment Lira saw the full breadth of him—the long coat of Nightfire House robes, torn at the edges, stained with soot and something darker. The armor underneath glimmered in pulses, each flicker revealing more detail: runes etched deep into the metal, symbols shaped like stars collapsing in on themselves.

“Nightfire warriors are meant to carry the flames,” he said quietly. “We are forged in fire so that others do not have to be.”

“And you?” Lira asked.

“And I carry more than my share.”

He faced her again, and the glow surged once more, stronger than before.

“It isn’t praise,” he continued. “It isn’t even a gift. It is a weight that devours its bearer slowly. A curse disguised as honor.”

The shadows around him trembled, reacting to the pulse of the glow. Lira felt a strange sensation—an awareness of something alive, something listening.

“You shouldn’t be standing so close,” Kael said.

“I heal,” Lira replied softly. “It’s what I do.”

“Then you should know better.” His eyes softened for a fraction of a heartbeat, as though he recognized something in her defiance. “My flame consumes those who touch it.”

“Then I won’t touch it,” she said. “But I will look.”

A corner of his mouth twitched—not quite a smile, not quite a grimace. Perhaps neither.

“And why would you risk it?” he asked.

“Because you look like someone who has been carrying a fire alone for far too long.”

His eyes darkened. The glow dimmed. For a moment, the corridor returned to its ordinary shadows.

Then he spoke again.

“Then you should know this,” Kael whispered. “I am not just a warrior cloaked in nightfire.”

He stepped closer, and the warmth rose again, curling around them like a living thing.

“I am a warrior bound by it.”

And with that, the glow surged once more, bright enough to blind, bright enough to burn, bright enough to remind Lira that some flames do not warm—they claim.

1.7 The Accidental Binding

A sudden warmth spread through Lira’s palm, so unexpected and sharp that it stole the breath from her lungs. She had only meant to steady the wounded man—an errant soldier found half-buried in the frost outside the infirmary. She had meant to soothe the torn flesh, nothing more. Healing was a habit, a reflex, something she’d been doing since childhood. But the moment her fingers brushed the soldier’s skin, something ancient and cold stirred beneath the surface, a vibration older than any spell she’d ever been taught.

The warmth turned to heat, then to something else—an electric pulse that raced up her arm and burst behind her eyes. Lira staggered back, clutching her hand. “What—?”

Before she could finish the question, the heat surged again, stronger, crackling, alive. The soldier’s body convulsed, not in pain but in a strange, almost reverent shudder. His eyes fluttered open, glowing a deep ember-red, like coals buried in ash.

“Don’t touch me,” he whispered, voice raw and trembling, though his grip tightened around her wrist with unexpected strength. “Don’t… please…”

But Lira wasn’t listening anymore. She felt it—something tightening inside her, wrapping around her pulse, binding her to the man with a force that felt both violent and intimate. The world dimmed, warping at the edges as if the air itself recoiled.

A thin line of crimson light formed between their hands. It wasn’t a beam or a spark. It resembled a thread—silken, trembling, humming with a rhythm that matched her heartbeat. She watched, frozen, as it throbbed and curled, weaving itself around her wrist and then around his.

“This isn’t possible,” Lira whispered. Her throat felt tight, constricted by fear. “This shouldn’t happen… healing magic doesn’t—”

The thread tightened.

A muffled crack echoed through the infirmary as though the room itself inhaled sharply. Candles guttered and sputtered out. Shadows curled inward, drawn toward the glowing line. And in that moment, the soldier’s eyes sharpened, the ember glow deepening until his pupils vanished entirely.

“Kael…” he murmured, almost in awe, as if speaking his own name for the first time. “House of Nightfire… bound again.”

Lira blinked. Bound? Again?

“Let go,” she pleaded, but her voice trembled. “I can’t,” Kael said. “Neither can you.”

The red thread constricted once more, then pulsed gently—like a heartbeat—before settling into a steady glow. Heat washed through Lira in waves, alternating between comfort and agony. She felt her bones hum, her skin prickle, her thoughts scatter as if caught in the wind.

Then, suddenly, silence.

The thread faded. The light dimmed. But the warmth remained—settled deep inside her, like a coal buried beneath the surface.

Lira dropped to her knees, gasping. “What… what have I done?”

Kael released her wrist slowly, his eyes flickering back to human tones, though a faint shimmer still lingered around them. “You have bound yourself,” he said quietly. “To me.”

“That’s impossible,” Lira whispered. “I don’t wield binding magic. I heal. That’s all I—”

“Then you do not understand what you touched.” Kael looked down at his own palm, where a faint scar glimmered—an old mark, half-faded, half-lit. “This brand has been dormant for decades. My family tried to destroy it, to silence it. But the curse… it awakens only when blood answers blood.”

“And my blood answered,” Lira murmured, stunned.

“A healer,” Kael said, almost with wonder. “A healer with forbidden magic. You shouldn’t exist anymore.”

Lira shook her head, trying to steady herself. “I’m no legend. I’m no omen. I’m just… a girl who heals the wounded.”

“Then you’re also the reason the bond returned.” His gaze sharpened, something hardening in it—fear, resentment, desperation, all layered together. “And now you share my fate.”

“What fate?” she demanded.

Kael inhaled sharply, as though bracing himself for a blow. “The fate of the Bloodbound. The warrior circle cursed to carry a legacy of blood and fire. We bind once… and never escape.”

Lira’s pulse throbbed in her ears. “You’re telling me I’m trapped? That this… this accident means I’m tied to you forever?”

“It means,” Kael corrected, “that until the curse is broken, you and I share a link. A link that hurts when one of us hurts. A link that strengthens when one of us grows. A link that cannot be severed by ordinary magic.”

Lira swallowed, trembling. “I don’t want this.”

Kael stepped back, tension etched into every line of his body. “Nor do I.”

His voice cracked slightly, revealing a vulnerability he desperately tried to hide. “I have spent my entire life resisting the bond. The curse burns through my veins every day. And now…” He looked at her again, a mixture of dread and awe. “Now it has chosen you.”

Lira stared at the faint warmth lingering on her palm. She could still feel it—subtle, humming, alive. A reminder of a moment she could never undo.

“I didn’t choose this,” she whispered.

“No,” Kael said softly, “but the bond did.”

And in the quiet aftermath, the flickering shadows creeping back into the room seemed to whisper the same truth.

Something ancient had awakened. Something dangerous had been set in motion. And two strangers, bound by blood, now stood at the center of it.
